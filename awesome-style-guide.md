# Awesome Style Guides


Style guides for every kind of communication, each entry individually retrieved and checked. The access tag is what keeps the list honest: paywalled standards belong here — they are the working reference in their fields — but they are never tagged as free.

**How this list was built.** Each section was produced by an automated research pipeline and passed a verification gate before landing here: every URL fetched in-run, every tag supported by quoted on-page evidence, and a proof-of-fetch check that a status code alone cannot satisfy. Sections that could not clear that gate are not here — see Coverage gaps.

Covers top-10 voice tips, global communications, and style decisions. For technical writers, developers, and all technology communicators at Microsoft and beyond. (free · Microsoft · 2026)

Covers tone, active voice, grammar rules, word usage, formatting, and accessibility. For software developers, technical writers, and documentation practitioners. (free · Google · 2026)

(free · Google · 2026)

For technical writers, UI writers, and content creators producing Apple documentation. (free · Apple · 2026)

## Presentations & Decks

- [UCSD Evidence-Based Presentation Design](https://multimedia.ucsd.edu/best-practices/presentation-design.html) — Specific, evidence-based prescriptive rules for slide design: use images instead of text when possible; use high-resolution, royalty-free images; no more than 4 lines of text per screen; 1.5 line spacing minimum; color contrast ratio ≥ 4.5; avoid animations; use consistent font family; align text and images; limit charts to one data series per slide. Grounded in Richard Mayer's Multimedia Learning and Stephen Kosslyn's *Clear and to the Point*. For instructors, academics, and anyone creating educational or professional slide presentations. (free · UCSD Instructional Media · 2020s)
- [Harvard T.H. Chan Slide Design Checklist](https://hsph.harvard.edu/research/health-communication/resources/slide-checklist/) — Structured prescriptive checklist organized by domain: Presentation Structure (1 slide per minute, sentence-form headlines, visual evidence), Layout (2–4 bullets max, continuous lines ≤ 2, generous blank space), Text (bold sans serif e.g. Calibri Bold; 28pt headline; 18–24pt body; ≥ 1.5 line spacing; contrast ratio ≥ 4.5), Visuals (consistent color scheme, inclusive imagery, high-resolution photos/illustrations not clipart, graphs use shapes and patterns not color alone). Prepared by Samuel Mendez. For health communicators, researchers, and public health professionals. (free · Harvard T.H. Chan School of Public Health, Center for Health Communication · 2020s)
- [MIT Broad Institute — Policy Presentation](https://mitcommlab.mit.edu/broad/commkit/policy-presentation/) — Prescriptive rules for academic-to-policy presentations: start with the main policy implication (not methods); keep jargon to a minimum; each slide title stands alone and conveys one message; figures show only the information needed to make the point; provide a clear blueprint for action; structure arguments in 1–3 groups. Developed with Dr. Dan Pomeroy of MIT's International Policy Lab. For researchers, scientists, and technical experts presenting to policymakers. (free · Broad Institute of MIT and Harvard · 2020s)
- [MIT NSE — Slide Design](https://mitcommlab.mit.edu/nse/commkit/slide-design/) — Prescriptive rules for scientific/technical slide design organized into four sections: Before You Start (determine purpose, audience, message; full-sentence titles containing the takeaway; support with evidence); Creating Slides (visuals > text; simplify figures; control information delivery); Maximize Effectiveness (increase signal-to-noise ratio; use annotations; check formatting; plan transitions); Quick Tips and Annotated Examples. For scientists and engineers at MIT NSE and elsewhere presenting technical results. (free · MIT NSE Communication Lab · 2020s)
- [Harvard Catalyst — Slides (Assertion-Evidence)](https://catalyst.harvard.edu/writing-communication-center/visualize-science/slides/) — Prescriptive rules for assertion-evidence slide design: begin each slide (except title) with a strong headline; incorporate strong visual evidence (photos, drawings, graphs, arranged words/equations as images); include clear captions/labels/symbols; minimize bullet points; limit animations and sounds; use appropriate number of slides (~1 per minute); maintain consistent look and feel; ensure readability from a distance; include inclusive images and language. Includes a downloadable Assertion Evidence Slides template, a self-assessment checklist, and a rubric. From Harvard Catalyst Writing & Communication Center, Visualize Science domain. (free · Harvard Catalyst · 2020s)
## Social media & short form

- [University of North Carolina Greensboro — Social Media Style Guide](https://uc.uncg.edu/social-media/social-media-style-guide/) — Prescriptive rules for writing and posting on social media at UNCG, covering voice and tone (Excellence, Opportunity, Impact), posting guidelines, general style rules (hashtags, emojis, images, exclamation points, link formatting), and network-specific guidelines for Facebook, Instagram, LinkedIn, X, YouTube, and TikTok. Covers account naming (college name first, avoid periods/underscores), writing style (short, smart, no "4" for "for", no "u" for "you"), hashtag policy (approved list only, no trending topics), engagement behavior (the "Bee Hive" and "Friendly Neighborhood Service Rep" personas), network-specific guidelines for Twitter (6-9 posts daily, shorten links, use & for "and"), Facebook (2-3 posts daily, use line breaks), YouTube (daily maintenance, descriptive names), Instagram (2 posts weekly, high-quality photos), and LinkedIn (1-2 posts weekly, job positions, alumni stories).
